How this works

Every CCAR-F task statement carries a weight. Miss a question and its statement's weight rises (×1.5, capped at 5.0); get it right and the weight falls (×0.7, floored at 0.5). The next question is drawn at random, biased by those weights and by the real exam's domain mix — so practice naturally concentrates on your weak spots and backs off as you master them.

The five most recently tested statements sit out each draw, so short runs stay varied. Progress persists between sessions; the weak-spots list shows where the tool currently thinks you should work.

In the desktop app, fresh questions are generated on demand through your local Claude Code. Opened as a plain page, questions come from the reviewed bank in questions.js instead.

When to flag a question

Flag — don't count — a generated question that looks flawed, so it can't skew your weights:

  • Invented specifics — the correct answer or an explanation leans on a flag, environment variable, or config behaviour you can't find in the exam guide or course (e.g. --non-interactive when the real flag is -p).
  • Two defensible answers — another option still looks right and the case against it rests on a preference or an unverifiable caveat, not a real correctness gap.
  • Outdated pattern as the answer — the "correct" option recommends a superseded mechanism (e.g. CLAUDE.local.md when @~/.claude/ imports are current practice).

Flagging fully discards the question — weights, accuracy, and history stay as if it never appeared. When in doubt, flag: a discarded good question costs one question; an absorbed bad one distorts your practice.
